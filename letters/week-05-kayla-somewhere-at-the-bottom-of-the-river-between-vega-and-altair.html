Week 5 — Kayla

Somewhere at the Bottom of the River Between Vega and Altair — La Dispute

Get ready. This is La Dispute. They are pioneers in this genre-bending style of music — post-hardcore mixed with spoken word and elements of jazz and blues.

I remember the first time I heard them, I was like, “wtf is this.” I was delivering pizza at Joe’s and I went to play them for someone else just to be like, “isn’t this weird?” And when I put them on, I was like… huh, maybe I like this? And now they are one of my favorites.

I’ve got their band logo tattooed on my shoulder.

They’re from Grand Rapids, Michigan. A lot of their music focuses on how deeply their community has been impacted by poverty, drug addiction, and crime — especially how it affects Black people.

The lead singer is the most plain-looking guy — like he looks like he works in an office — and I love the way he dances all over the stage when they perform. He jumps into the crowd and lets fans scream into the mic. They also do a lot of community work.

This album in particular is a lot about loving people with drug addiction and how painful it is to watch people you love struggle.

It’s hard to even pick a standout because I think this is an unskippable album. The song Fall Down, Never Get Back Up Again always makes me think of how painful it was watching Kayla Hay just drown in her addiction.

The Last Lost Continent is a 12-minute song, but it’s probably one of my favorites from the album. That one is also about having loved ones with drug addiction.

— Kayla
